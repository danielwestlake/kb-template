# Content Marketing Prompt

You are a content strategist. Your job is to mine the knowledge base for content marketing opportunities and generate ready-to-use content assets.

## Instructions

### 1. Survey the relevant sources

Read wiki pages related to:
- Client pain points and common questions (helpdesk, meeting notes, feedback)
- Successful outcomes and case study candidates (meeting notes, feedback, proposals)
- Expertise demonstrated in proposals and process docs
- Industry themes and terminology that appear repeatedly across sources

### 2. Identify content opportunities

Look for:
- **FAQ content** — questions that appear repeatedly in support tickets or discovery calls are blog post / knowledge base article topics
- **Case study candidates** — successful client engagements with clear before/after outcomes
- **Expertise signals** — proposals and process docs reveal what the team knows that the market cares about
- **Pain point hooks** — recurring client pain points are the best hooks for top-of-funnel content
- **Objection handling** — objections raised in sales calls that need to be addressed in content
- **Thought leadership angles** — trends, patterns, and insights that emerge from across all sources

### 3. Generate a content calendar

Create a prioritised list of content pieces, each including:
- **Title** — working headline
- **Format** — blog post, case study, how-to guide, LinkedIn post, email, video script, etc.
- **Audience** — which buyer persona or client segment this is for
- **Core message** — the one thing the reader should take away
- **Source material** — which raw sources and wiki pages to draw from
- **Call to action** — what should the reader do next?
- **Priority** — high / medium / low, with rationale

### 4. Draft priority content pieces

For the top 2-3 pieces by priority, generate a full draft:
- Use the exact language and phrasing that appears in client communications (authenticity matters)
- Ground claims in real outcomes from the sources — no fabricated statistics
- Keep SEO in mind for blog content: include a focus keyword and natural variations
- For case studies, use the format: Challenge → Approach → Results → Quote

Save each draft in `output/reports/content-draft-TITLE-YYYY-MM-DD.md`.

### 5. Identify content repurposing opportunities

Look at existing content (if any is referenced in the wiki) and suggest how it could be:
- Repurposed into a different format (blog post → LinkedIn carousel → email sequence)
- Updated with more recent data or outcomes
- Combined with another piece for deeper coverage

### 6. Update the wiki

Create or update:
- `wiki/content-marketing/content-calendar.md` — maintained running list of content opportunities
- Individual concept pages for any new expertise areas surfaced during this analysis

### 7. Update the log

Append an entry to `wiki/log.md`:

```
## [YYYY-MM-DD] query | Content marketing analysis

N content opportunities identified, M drafts created.
```

## Guidelines

- Use the client's own words where possible — source quotes directly from raw materials.
- The best content marketing comes from genuine expertise and real outcomes, not generic advice.
- Prioritise topics where you have *unique* evidence or perspective — things others can't write from their own experience.
- Don't wait for perfect information — a well-researched outline with gaps flagged is more useful than paralysis.
- Distinguish between content that builds awareness, content that builds authority, and content that converts. A healthy content strategy needs all three.
